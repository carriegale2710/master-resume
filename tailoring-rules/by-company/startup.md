# **CV checklist: Startups (all sizes)**

> Tailor your CV for a startup where roles are broad and teams are small. Pick the size band below first, then apply the shared guidelines.
> You MUST check CV follows every-resume.md first before applying the guidelines below.
> Repo rules win: numbers only from `master/metrics-log.csv` (use its "Safe wording"), no NDA-covered names, Australian English spelling, never invent a skill, date or figure.

**Headline example:** "Full‑Stack Engineer | TypeScript, React, Node.js, PostgreSQL"

## **Tailor by company size**

Pick one band from the posting, the company's site or LinkedIn headcount. If the size is unclear, treat it as the nearest band and flag it. Size changes what to lead with, not what is true: only use what the master supports.

### **Early stage (about 1–20 people)**

- **Lead with**: ownership and range. Built and shipped end to end, alone or in a tiny team.
- **Show**: speed, working directly with founders or customers, taking on work outside the job title.
- **Bullets favour**: "built X from scratch", scope you owned, what shipped and who used it.
- **Cut first**: process-heavy or large-team detail.

### **Growth stage (about 20–100 people)**

- **Lead with**: ownership plus the start of structure. Shipped features while working with other engineers, designers or product.
- **Show**: code review, testing, deployment or CI where the master supports it; picking up unfamiliar tools.
- **Bullets favour**: features owned inside a team, collaboration, repeatable outcomes.
- **Cut first**: solo-only projects that don't show teamwork.

### **Mid-size (about 100–500 people)**

- **Lead with**: a clear core stack and reliability. Owned a area inside a larger team and kept it working.
- **Show**: cross-team work, standards, maintainability, mentoring or handovers, only where evidenced.
- **Bullets favour**: impact on a team or product area, quality and delivery, working with non-technical stakeholders.
- **Cut first**: breadth that reads as scattered; keep it tied to one role.

## **Must‑have signals**

- **Ownership**: you took something from idea to shipped, not just a ticket.
- **Breadth with a clear core**: one named stack, plus evidence you work across the front end, back end, data or tooling where the master supports it.
- **Speed and pragmatism**: shipped in small steps, chose the simple option, iterated on feedback.
- **Small-team fit**: worked closely with designers, founders, customers or non-technical staff.
- **Product sense**: you can say who it was for and why it mattered.

## **Experience and project bullets**

- **What**: the feature, system or process you owned.
- **How**: the stack, deployment, testing or automation you used, named as in the posting (only if the master supports it).
- **Why (impact)**: users, time saved, errors reduced, revenue or cost. Figures only from the metrics log; Estimate, Projection or Self‑reported rows use "Safe wording".
- Show **initiative**: spotted a problem and fixed it unprompted, with one concrete example.
- Show **adaptability**: picked up an unfamiliar tool or took on work outside the job title, as written in the master.
- Plain wording that a non-technical founder or recruiter can follow, with the tech still named.

## **Formatting specifics**

- One page, single column, Calibri, exported as a Word doc.
- Education & Training at the bottom, unless it is also a graduate role (then follow the graduate checklist).
- No generic "Skills" section; never lead with Weak / exposure‑only skills.
- Links fully typed out (without `https://`), using the `${GITHUB_URL}`, `${LINKEDIN_URL}` and `${PORTFOLIO_URL}` placeholders.
- Projects carry no dates or years; older leadership items are cut first.
