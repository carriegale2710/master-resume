# **No-bullshit CV checklist specific by the role (UX/Design)**

> Tailor your CV for the specific design role you are applying for.
> You MUST check CV follows every-resume.md first before applying the role-specific guidelines below.

## **5\. Product Designer (Junior / UX/UI)**

**Headline example:** “Junior Product Designer | UX Research, Interaction Design, Figma”

### **Must‑have signals**

- **Product design focus is clear**: research → concepts → UI → handoff.
- **2–3 case studies** showing full process, not just pretty screens.

### **Case study / project bullets**

For each featured project (in CV or portfolio summary):

- **What**: problem, users, constraints, your role.
- **How**:
  - Research methods (interviews, surveys, usability tests)
  - Key insights
  - Ideation, wireframes, prototypes, iterations
  - Handoff to devs (specs, components)
- **Why (impact)**:
  - “Increased task success rate from 58% → 87%”
  - “Reduced time‑on‑task by 30%”
  - “Improved conversion by 15% after checkout redesign”
- Show **collaboration**: working with PMs, engineers, stakeholders.

### **Experience / internships**

- Emphasize design work: research, flows, UI, design systems, usability testing.
- Quantify outcomes where possible (metrics, stakeholder feedback, adoption).

### **Portfolio requirements**

- **2–3 full case studies**, each: problem, your role, process, final design, outcome.
- Show **research to concept to UI to handoff**; include sketches or wireframes, not just final screens.
- State **what you did versus the team**, in plain words.
- At least one **interactive prototype** link or short walkthrough.
- Outcomes use only evidenced figures; otherwise describe the feedback or decision.

### **Formatting specifics**

- CV points to a **strong portfolio**; portfolio does the visual heavy lifting.
- CV itself stays clean and text‑focused; no big images or multi‑column layouts.

## **6\. Service Designer (Junior)**

**Headline example:** “Junior Service Designer | Journey Mapping, Blueprinting, Co‑Design”

### **Must‑have signals**

- **Service design focus is explicit**: end‑to‑end journeys, touchpoints, ops, policy.
- **2–3 service projects** showing:
  - Stakeholder mapping
  - Journey maps \+ service blueprints
  - Co‑design workshops
  - Implementation/impact

### **Project bullets**

- **What**: service, users, channels (digital \+ offline), constraints.
- **How**:
  - Research (contextual inquiry, interviews, shadowing)
  - Mapping (journeys, blueprints, ecosystems)
  - Co‑design sessions, prototypes, pilots
  - Handoff to product/ops/policy teams
- **Why (impact)**:
  - “Reduced average handling time by 20%”
  - “Cut drop‑off at onboarding step 3 from 35% → 12%”
  - “Enabled new channel (SMS) serving 10k+ users/month”
- Show **systems thinking**: frontstage/backstage, policies, constraints, org impact.

### **Experience / internships**

- Highlight work with cross‑functional teams, operations, policy, or customer service.
- Quantify improvements in KPIs, NPS, cost, time, error rates.

### **Portfolio requirements**

- **2–3 service case studies** with a journey map and a service blueprint shown (redacted if needed).
- Show **stakeholder mapping, research and a co‑design workshop** (photos or artefacts, no identifiable participants).
- Show **frontstage and backstage**: how the idea would actually run.
- Include **implementation or pilot evidence**, or say plainly that it was a concept.
- State your role versus the team.

### **Formatting specifics**

- CV summarizes projects; portfolio/case studies show maps/blueprints.
- Keep CV to one page (two maximum), text‑first; no complex diagrams in the CV itself.

## **How the design roles differ**

Pick one role and headline it. Don't blend them: the posting's wording tells you which one it is.

| Role            | Core question                                | Owns                                                                 | Evidence to lead with                        |
| --------------- | -------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| UX/UI Designer  | How does it work _and_ look?                 | Flows, wireframes, interface, prototypes                             | End-to-end case study, flows to final UI     |
| UI Designer     | How does the interface look and behave?      | Components, layout, type, colour, design system, states              | Polished screens, component library, handoff |
| Visual Designer | How does it communicate?                     | Brand, graphics, illustration, layout, campaign and marketing assets | Brand and visual work, craft, consistency    |
| UX Researcher   | What do users need, and what's the evidence? | Research plans, interviews, surveys, usability tests, synthesis      | Method choice, insights, decisions changed   |

Quick split: **UX/UI** spans both halves, **UI** is interface craft, **Visual** is brand and communication beyond product screens, **Research** produces evidence rather than designs.

Repo rules apply to every role below: figures only from `master/metrics-log.csv` (the examples are illustrative), no NDA-covered names, never invent a project, tool or result.

## **7\. UX/UI Designer (Junior)**

**Headline example:** “Junior UX/UI Designer | User Flows, Wireframing, Prototyping, Figma”

### **Must‑have signals**

- **Both halves shown**: UX (flows, structure, usability) and UI (visual interface).
- **2–3 case studies** from problem to final screens.
- Differs from Product Designer by **less emphasis on strategy and metrics, more on flows and interface**.

### **Case study / project bullets**

- **What**: problem, users, your role.
- **How**: user flows, wireframes, prototypes, usability checks, then visual design and handoff.
- **Why (impact)**: task success, time‑on‑task or usability feedback, only where evidenced.
- Show **before and after** of a flow, not only final screens.

### **Portfolio requirements**

- **2–3 case studies** that each cover both UX (flows, wireframes, testing) and final UI.
- Show **at least one before and after flow**.
- **Clickable prototype** for at least one project.
- Mobile and desktop (or the platform in the posting) where relevant.
- State your role versus the team.

### **Formatting specifics**

- Portfolio link prominent; CV stays text‑first.

## **8\. UI Designer (Junior)**

**Headline example:** “Junior UI Designer | Visual Interface Design, Design Systems, Figma”

### **Must‑have signals**

- **Interface craft is the focus**: layout, typography, colour, spacing, hierarchy, states.
- **Component or design‑system work**: reusable components, tokens, variants.
- **Responsive and accessible** screens (contrast, tap targets, text sizes).

### **Case study / project bullets**

- **What**: product, platforms and the screens you owned.
- **How**: style guides, component libraries, interaction states, motion, developer handoff (specs, annotations).
- **Why (impact)**: consistency, faster build, fewer handoff questions, only where evidenced.
- Don't claim research or strategy work you didn't do.

### **Portfolio requirements**

- **2–3 projects** with high‑fidelity screens across key states (empty, error, loading, hover).
- A **component sheet or mini design system** (type scale, colour, spacing, components).
- Show **responsive layouts** and a visible accessibility consideration (contrast, text size).
- A **handoff example**: specs, annotations or a dev‑ready file.
- Optional: micro‑interaction or motion samples.

### **Formatting specifics**

- Portfolio shows polished screens and a component sheet; CV lists the tools used inside bullets (no skills grid).

## **9\. Visual Designer (Junior)**

**Headline example:** “Junior Visual Designer | Brand Identity, Layout, Illustration, Adobe Creative Suite”

### **Must‑have signals**

- **Communication and brand focus**: identity, campaigns, social, print, illustration, motion or presentation design.
- **Craft shown**: typography, colour, composition and consistency across assets.
- **Tools named as in the posting** (e.g. Adobe Illustrator, Photoshop, InDesign, Figma), only if the master supports them.

### **Project bullets**

- **What**: brief, audience and the deliverables you produced.
- **How**: concept, iterations, feedback rounds, production files and delivery formats.
- **Why (impact)**: engagement, recognition or client or stakeholder feedback, only where evidenced.
- Show **range within a consistent system**, not unrelated one‑offs.

### **Portfolio requirements**

- **Range within a consistent standard**: identity, layout, illustration, social or campaign pieces.
- At least one **full brand or campaign project** with the brief, process and applications (mockups).
- Show **process**: sketches, concepts, iterations, then final.
- Deliverables in **real‑world context** (mockups, formats, sizes).
- Only work you made or clearly credited; no stock or client assets you can't show.

### **Formatting specifics**

- Portfolio does the visual work; CV stays plain, single column.

## **10\. UX Researcher (Junior)**

**Headline example:** “Junior UX Researcher | Usability Testing, User Interviews, Survey Design, Synthesis”

### **Must‑have signals**

- **Method range with reasons**: why you chose interviews, surveys, usability tests or diary studies.
- **Evidence to decision**: what you found and what changed because of it.
- **Rigour**: sample, recruitment, bias awareness, ethics and consent, kept at a plain‑language level.

### **Project bullets**

- **What**: research question, users, constraints, your role.
- **How**: study design, recruitment, moderation, analysis (affinity mapping, thematic analysis), reporting.
- **Why (impact)**: the design or product decision it informed, with a figure only if logged.
- Show **communication**: readouts, workshops, insight summaries for non‑researchers.

### **Portfolio requirements**

- **2–3 research case studies**, each: question, method and why, participants (anonymised), findings, what changed.
- **Research artefacts** (redacted): discussion guide, survey, affinity map, journey map, insight report.
- At least **one qualitative and one quantitative method** shown, or a stated reason why not.
- A **readout or summary** written for non‑researchers.
- No participant names, recordings or NDA‑covered client details.

### **Formatting specifics**

- Portfolio uses redacted case studies or research reports; name no participants or NDA‑covered clients.
- CV stays text‑first, with methods and tools (e.g. Dovetail, Maze, Figma) inside bullets only where the master supports them.
